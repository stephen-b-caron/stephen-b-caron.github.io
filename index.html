About

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Biology at New York University, working at the intersection of forest ecology and biogeochemistry. My research asks how global change drivers—including climate warming, nitrogen deposition, elevated atmospheric CO₂, and emerging forest pathogens—reshape the structure and functioning of temperate forest ecosystems. I am particularly interested in how these stressors interact across scales to alter nutrient cycling, carbon storage, and vegetation dynamics. I approach these questions through long-term field observations, manipulative experiments, and spatial analysis. Research Interests

Forest Biogeochemistry

I study how forests regulate the cycling of carbon, nitrogen, and other nutrients through vegetation, soil, and the atmosphere, and how these cycles shift in response to environmental stressors operating at local and regional scales.

Global Change Ecology

A central focus of my work is understanding how multiple, co-occurring global change factors—warming, altered precipitation, CO₂ enrichment, and biological invasions—interact to drive changes in forest community composition and ecosystem processes.

Nutrient Cycling & Soil Ecology

I am interested in plant–soil feedbacks that regulate nutrient availability in forest systems, including the roles of microbial community dynamics, organic matter decomposition, and belowground carbon allocation.

Ecosystem Structure & Function

My research examines how disturbances, succession, and stressor-driven mortality alter forest structure, and how those structural changes propagate into measurable shifts in ecosystem function, productivity, and resilience.
